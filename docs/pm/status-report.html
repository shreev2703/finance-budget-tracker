3. Are we on schedule?

Yes. 93.6% of the scope is done with two working days left, and every user story (US-01 to US-09, plus the stretch goal US-10) is built, tested, and merged. 11.5 estimated hours remain.

  • Scope grew 40 h in the final sprint (Nov 16): fixing the tests the Supabase switch broke, all four TE7 test levels, account settings, the Plaid bank import, deployment, and the final documents. 28.5 h of that is already done.
  • Every sprint before this one finished all of its tickets. Estimates have held: 169 estimated hours took 169.5 actual hours (cost performance index 1.00). The biggest miss was #44 (transactions page: 2 h estimated, 5 h actual).
  • Workload is uneven. 111.5 of 169.5 hours (66%) are Shreev’s; Juan S. and Juan F. logged none in the final sprint so far. To rebalance, Shreev took over the test fixes and the stretch goal, while Juan S. owns deployment and Juan F. the TE5 review. A Thursday check-in moves anything not started to whoever is free.

Risks to the Nov 20 finish

  • Deployment (#93, 3 h, Juan S.) hasn’t started. If it slips, the app is still fully usable locally and in demo mode, but the SRD has no public URL.
  • The team’s Supabase connection string is only on Juan S.’s laptop. Until it is shared, nobody else can run the app against the live database (#87).
  • TE5 reviews (#65, #66, 1.5 h) are unstarted. Small, but they are graded.

4. When will the project be complete?

We forecast from velocity: estimated hours completed per day.

  • Final-sprint pace: 28.5 h in 3 days, about 9.5 h/day. At that pace the remaining 11.5 h take about 1.2 days, which means Thursday, Nov 19, one day before the deadline.
  • Whole-project pace: 169 h over 9.3 weeks, about 18.2 h/week. At that slower pace the remaining work takes under a week, but the deadline caps it: anything not done Friday is dropped, not delayed.
  • Forecast: complete on Fri, Nov 20, if Juan S. deploys and shares the database by Thursday. If not, those two items ship without a public URL, and everything graded still lands on time.

6. How we report, and what we changed on the board

  • Source of truth: the GitHub project “Finance Budget Tracker Board.” Every ticket has an owner, an Estimated Time (hrs), an Actual Time (hrs) once done, a status, and a milestone.
  • New field: Iteration. We added a dated Iteration field with all eight sprints (start date and length) and set it on all 75 tickets. That lets GitHub group hours by sprint and draw the burnup over real dates. The old Sprint labels had no dates, and four tickets had none.
  • Fixed on the board: tickets with no sprint (#22, #40, #85, #94) now have one; the leftover TE5 reviews (#65, #66) moved into the final sprint; the finished bank-import ticket (#94) moved to Done; finished milestones were closed.
  • Charts in GitHub: the board’s Insights tab can show the same two charts live: Burn up filtered to all items, and a bar chart with X-axis = Iteration, group by Assignees, Y-axis = sum of Actual Time (hrs).
  • Weekly cadence: at the end of each sprint, the PM exports the board, re-runs docs/pm/compute.cjs and build-report.cjs, and brings this page to the retro: are we on schedule, when will we finish, what has it cost.
